Security-by-Design for Distributed Agentic AI

Why security must become an architectural property before autonomous systems are allowed to act

Aridio Silva
Independent Researcher · Creator of SGAEIA

Published 2026-09-17 · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22816239

Abstract

Agentic AI changes the security problem because artificial intelligence is no longer limited to producing text, classifications, or recommendations. Autonomous agents can hold identities, invoke tools, access resources, delegate tasks, coordinate with other agents, and trigger actions across distributed edge–cloud environments. In that setting, conventional application security controls remain necessary but are no longer sufficient. This article argues that security for distributed agentic AI must be established as an architectural property before execution, rather than added later as a collection of filters, prompt guardrails, monitoring tools, or post-hoc compliance checks. It develops a Security-by-Design perspective around bounded authority, authenticated delegation, Zero Trust, revocability, continuous governance, and verifiable evidence. These properties form part of the research foundation of SGAEIA — Secure Governed Autonomous Edge Intelligence Architecture. ---
